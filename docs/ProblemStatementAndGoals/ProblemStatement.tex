\section{Problem Statement}
Visually impaired individuals can face many difficulties when navigating screen-based websites and
applications that rely on visual feedback. These barriers become especially
challenging when completing complex, multi-step digital tasks that require the interpretation of changing
system states. This project aims to support independent task completion while allowing users to
remain informed of relevant system changes and maintain control when completing digital work.

\subsection{Problem}
Visually impaired individuals may encounter significant barriers when
completing computer tasks, as many websites and applications require visual interactions
to communicate information, present possible actions, and indicate changes in a system's state.
Complex and unfamiliar tasks can require users to navigate multiple applications, interpret changing
interface and application states, recover from errors and unexpected outcomes, and determine if actions
were completed successfully. Managing these tasks can be particularly difficult without relying on
visual feedback.

Barriers like these can make it difficult for users to complete multi-step tasks independently and
to remain aware of what's happening while a task is being performed. This problem has become increasingly
important as many activities depend more on websites, applications, and other digital services.
Difficulties with nonvisual interactions can therefore limit many users' abilities to interact with
the modern digital world.

\subsection{Inputs and Outputs}
The intended input is the individual's voice as a form of control.
Spoken input includes goals, preferences, clarification answers,
corrections, approvals, and stop requests. Users who are able may also supply
keyboard or text input for assistance.

The intended outputs are actions on permitted services or the computer,
concise spoken progress and results, requests for clarification or
approval, and an accessible, reviewable task history. Completion feedback
must distinguish verified results, partial work, and unresolved failures.

\subsection{Stakeholders}
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[Visually impaired individuals]
Primary stakeholders are visually impaired, English-speaking individuals who want to complete digital
tasks while retaining control through non-visual interactions. Initial evaluation
will recruit blind Mac users with screen-reader experience; the broader
stakeholder group does not imply evaluation of every user population.
\item[Users Wanting Non-Visual Interactions]
Secondary stakeholders are non-visually impaired individuals who still want the ability to complete
digital tasks without relying on visual feedback. They may be unable to view their computer screens
for other reasons, or may simply have a desire not to rely on their screen.
\end{description}

\subsection{Environment}
The expected environment is a Mac computer running macOS with Google Chrome,
a microphone, audio output, and internet access. Some tasks may also require access to the local Mac
environment. Users may also have existing accessibility tools like VoiceOver and keyboard navigation enabled.

\section{Goals}
The Development Plan, Section 9.3, specifies provisional targets and the
evaluation method for these goals. The goals below describe intended outcomes,
not achieved results.

\begin{enumerate}[label=G\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Complete complex digital tasks independently.}
Users should be able to complete common, multi-step tasks across websites and applications without
relying on visual feedback or sighted assistance. Success can be measured by task completion rate
and the amount of sighted assistance that is required.

\item \textbf{Maintain awareness and control during digital tasks.}
Users should be able to understand relevant task progress and state changes, provide input when required,
and stop or redirect tasks when necessary. Measure successful corrections, explicit stop acknowledgment and execution-stop
confirmation, and approval before consequential actions. Clarification counts
provide context; fewer questions alone do not establish better control.

\item \textbf{Accurately receive results and execute commands.}
Users should be able to have confidence that the program is correctly tracking the system's state,
retrieving results, and executing their desired actions and commands. Compare reported outcomes and executed actions against independently verified
application state and the user's request. Report incorrect or unsupported
completion claims separately from successful actions.

\item \textbf{Recover from errors and unexpected outcomes.}
Users should be able to identify unexpected, misunderstood, or failed actions and recover without restarting
the entire task or relying on sighted assistance. Measure whether recovery succeeds without sighted help or a full restart,
along with recovery time and clarification turns.

\item \textbf{Provide a straightforward and reliable setup process.}
Individuals responsible for setting up the interface should have a smooth setup process.
Routine use after initial setup should not require assistance from the setup helper. Success can be measured by the number of clicks or commands needed,
the setup time before the program can be used, as well as how many times the setup helper is required to assist after installation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Stretch Goals}
\begin{enumerate}[label=SG\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Support additional applications and operating systems.}
If time permits, it would be beneficial to extend the implementation to multiple browsers and operating systems to make the system accessible to more users.

\item \textbf{Support a second voice channel.}
A useful extension is to allow users to access the same task context through their computer as well as their cell phone. This extension
could allow them to interact with their computer through voice-controlled interactions without being directly in front of it.

\item \textbf{Support saving workflows.}
Allowing users to save common workflows, such as paying one's credit card bill, would be helpful and improve their efficiency in
interacting with the computer. Users could have the option to complete a task or interaction and save it to a collection of frequent workflows
that they can quickly request when desired. Saving a workflow does not grant
standing approval: consequential actions still require confirmation of their
current details.

\end{enumerate}

\section*{Appendix A: Reflection}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{What went well while writing this deliverable?}
Overall, the team communication was well-established and effective. There was consistent communication
within the group's Discord server about the deliverable, how the initial draft would be completed, and how review would be
handled. The process of using Git for branches and GitHub Pull Requests to foster discussion on changes was also organized
and easy to manage.

\item \textbf{What pain points arose and how were they resolved?}
The group had initially determined that transparently using AI assistance for writing the draft of the document before manually
reviewing would be effective and efficient, but Curtis found this to be rather time-consuming when it came down to the review
process. Some sections didn't fully align with expectations, were slightly difficult to understand, and were rather vague due to the nature of LLM's and
their writing style, so the next process to try moving forward will be primarily manual writing first, then use AI assistance to proofread and
expand on anything needing elaboration. This should be beneficial as a whole for the group's effectiveness when it comes to writing
proposals and further documents.

\item \textbf{How did the team adjust scope?}
During the team's planning, the scope was limited to only the visually impaired, as well as only handling web page interactions.
After a conversation over email with Dr. Spencer Smith, the scope was suggested to be expanded to general users who might desire the
project's functionality, as well as ensuring to handle more applications and complex state changes than just creating a simple screen reader.
After meeting with Teaching Assistant Rebecca DiFilippo, this scope was confirmed as sufficient as of the initial time of writing; the team
was reminded, however, to be open to further widening the scope or limiting the scope to a small subset of tasks, according to how the development process moves along.

\end{enumerate}
