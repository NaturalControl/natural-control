\item \textbf{What went well while writing this deliverable?}

One thing that went well while writing the SRS was how our team divided up the responsibilities. Aaron and I were responsible for the System section, while other team members worked on sections such as the goals and Hazard Analysis. This allowed us to focus on specific parts of the document and made the workload more manageable.

Another thing that worked well was using AI tools to help review our work and identify potential issues. These tools helped us check whether our requirements were complete, consistent, and aligned with other sections of the document. I also spent time reviewing the requirements to make sure they were realistic and feasible for our project. During this process, we identified and resolved several discrepancies between the SRS and Hazard Analysis. Overall, I think dividing responsibilities and reviewing each other's work helped us produce a clearer and more consistent deliverable.

\item \textbf{What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and how did you resolve them?}

One of the main challenges I experienced while working on the SRS was dealing with inconsistencies between the SRS and Hazard Analysis. Since different team members were responsible for different sections and documents, some requirements ended up describing slightly different system behaviors. To resolve this, I compared the requirements in both documents and reviewed which approaches were more realistic, feasible, and appropriate for our project. This helped us make decisions about how the system should handle situations such as task interruptions, user approvals, and failures.

Another challenge was making sure that our requirements were specific enough to implement and test. Some requirements were initially too broad or difficult to guarantee, so I worked on refining them to better reflect what our team could realistically develop. I also helped review the relationships between requirements and project goals to make sure the document was consistent and that important safety requirements were not overlooked.

Overall, these challenges taught me that writing requirements is not just about describing what we want the system to do, but also making sure that the requirements are achievable, clear, and consistent with the rest of the project.

\item \textbf{How many of your requirements were inspired by speaking to clients or their proxies?}

I did not personally participate in any client or stakeholder interviews while working on the SRS. Most of the requirements I worked on were developed through team discussions, where we collectively considered the needs and challenges that blind users might face when interacting with our system. We used these discussions, along with our project goals and technical considerations, to determine what features and safety measures would be important.

Although some of our requirements may have been influenced by stakeholder feedback gathered by other team members, I cannot confidently identify an exact number that came directly from those conversations. Many of our initial requirements were based on assumptions that we believe are reasonable but still need to validate with actual users.

Moving forward, I think gathering more direct feedback from blind users will be important to confirm that our requirements address their needs and to identify anything we may have overlooked.

\item \textbf{Which courses will help your team succeed with the capstone project?}

I think Software Requirements, Human-Computer Interfaces, and Software Testing are three of the most useful courses we have taken that will help our team succeed with this capstone project.

Software Requirements was particularly helpful when developing our SRS document, since it taught us how to identify, organize, and write clear requirements for a software system. Human-Computer Interfaces is also very relevant to our project because Natural Control is designed primarily for blind users. The concepts we learned in that course can help us design an interface that is accessible, easy to navigate, and suitable for users who rely on voice interaction.

Finally, Software Testing will help us verify that our system behaves as expected and that the features we implement meet the requirements we have defined. This will be especially important for testing safety-related features such as user approvals, task interruptions, and error handling.

Overall, I think these courses provide a strong foundation for designing, implementing, and evaluating Natural Control.
