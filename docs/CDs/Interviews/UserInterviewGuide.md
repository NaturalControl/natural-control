# User Interview Guide:

## Interview Tips

- Ask about real experiences and recent examples before discussing possible product ideas.
- Use the main questions as prompts, not a script that must be read word for word. Questions can be tailored to the interviewee's experience.
- Ask one question at a time. Let the participant finish, and follow up on details that are relevant to the research goals.
- Do not assume that a participant uses a screen reader, wants a voice assistant, or finds a particular task difficult.
- If a participant has not used a particular kind of tool, do not pressure them to speculate about it.
- Adapt the session to the participant's preferred communication method and pace.
- Prefer “Tell me about the last time…” over “Would you use…” or “Would it be helpful if…”.
- Use neutral probes: “What happened next?”, “Can you tell me more?”, “How did you handle that?”, and “What do you mean by that?”

## Before the interview

### Accessibility and consent

Ask the participant what would make the session accessible and comfortable. For example:

- What format works best for sharing information before and during the session?
- Are there assistive technologies, devices, or settings you would like us to accommodate?
- Would you like breaks, a support person, or any other arrangement?

Explain the purpose of the session, how notes or recordings will be used (audio and/or transcript), that their interview will be anonymized in our documents. __Explicitly ask for consent before beginning the interview.__ 


### Suggested introduction

“Thanks for speaking with us. We are a group of fourth-year software engineering students exploring how people use computers and other digital tools in everyday life. We’re learning what works well, and where things can be frustrating to help us with our final design project. This isn't a test; there are no right or wrong answers. We’re interested in your experiences, including things that do not work as expected. You can skip any question or stop whenever you like. ”

## Interview questions


### Q1: Tell me a bit about yourself and how technology fits into your day. As much as you’re comfortable sharing, how does your disability affect how you use a computer, and has that changed over time?
- Get some background info about the user. Tailor this question slightly to the user being interviewed. 

### Q2: Could you walk me through what tasks you might typically use a computer for?
Possible follow-ups:
- What devices do you use most often?
- Where or when do you usually use them?
Examples of tasks if required: creating files and documents, banking, shopping, forms, appointments, news, work software, entertainment, emails

### Q3:  What accessibility tools or features or voice assistants do you use most often, and what do they do?
Possible follow-ups:
-Do these things do the task for you or help you do it?

Do not list products immediately. If the participant wants examples, you can ask about screen readers, magnification, speech recognition, or built-in accessibility features.

### Q4: How have these tools been working for you?

Possible follow-ups:
- What do you find works particularly well?
- Are there situations where they are less helpful/could be improved?
- What makes a tool feel empowering versus stressful or frustrating?

### Q5: Walk me through how you tell your computer what to do, for example opening an app, clicking a button, or typing a message
Possible follow-ups:
- What aspects of the computer are hardest to use (e.g. certain websites, computer settings...)

### Q6: Think about the last time you had difficulty completing something on a computer. What were you trying to do, and what happened?
Possible follow-ups:
- Where did things become difficult?
- What did you try next?
- Did you find a workaround or ask someone for help?
- How did the situation end?

### Q7: If you need help with a computer task, such as completing an action or setting up a tool, what do you usually do? 

Possible follow-ups:
- What do you try on your own first?
- Who or what do you turn to for help?
- How do you feel about asking for help?
- How long do you usually have to wait for help (e.g. is help available immediately, or only once a week)?

### Q8: When you’re doing a task, how does your computer let you know what’s happening, like whether it understood you, what’s on the screen, or whether something worked?
Possible follow-ups:
- Please give a specific example of this.
- Please describe the quantity of information given, is there typically, too much, too little, or just enough information given?
- Was any information missing, confusing, or repeated?
If the participant asks for clarification, can say spoken feedback if applicable.

### Q9: Think about the last time you did something online that mattered to you, such as sending an important email, submitting a form, or making a purchase. How did you make sure it was right before you finished? 
Possible follow-ups: 
- What did you check, and how did you check it? 
- Was anything difficult to check or confirm? 
- Have you ever found out afterward that something was wrong? What happened?

### Q10: Can you tell me about a time a website, app, or accessibility tool did something unexpected while you were using it. What happened, and how did you fix it?
If the user needs a nudge: message to wrong person, deleted wrong file, accidentally changed a setting
Possible follow-ups:
- What would have made the situation easier to recover from?
- How did you realize something had gone wrong?


### Q11: Are there any things you would like to do on a computer that you currently avoid or have someone else do? Why?
Possible follow-up:
- What would need to change for you to do that yourself?

### Q12: Is there anything else you would like to share that would be important about understanding your experience using a computer? 
Possible follow-up:
- Is there anything you wish designers or developers understood better?
- Are there any major pain points or pleasure points when working on a computer that you would like share with us?

## Closing and optional recruitment

Thank the participant and ask whether they have questions or feedback about the session. 
Can give a bit more of a detailed description about the project. Ask them whether they would be willing to test out a prototype when we create it in a couple of months.
