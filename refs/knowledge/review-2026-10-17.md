## Findings from the initial pass (see resolution update below)

1. **Secrets:** E.3 prohibits secrets through voice, while FR23 permits spoken one-time codes as an explicit fallback. Decide the exception and disclose the speech-provider exposure; avoid claiming credentials never reach the conversation if this route exists.
2. **Consent actions:** FR9 and FR.Sa.13 require approval for anything not read-only, but FR24 automatically rejects cookies. Rejection changes browser state. Define the narrowly allowed exception or require approval; make the classification consistent in both documents.
3. **Progress and loop limits:** FR5 gives one reminder and traces to FR.Sa.11 (a step/time limit). NFR.Sa.3 instead calls for updates after an interval without progress. Separate the reminder rule, ongoing progress rule, and execution limit, with distinct trace links and constants.
4. **Stopping state:** S.9 excludes Stopping from active states while P6 constrains only active tasks. The diagram visually places Stopping inside Active. Align the definition and diagram so a second task cannot start while earlier actions settle. Specify bare-stop and correction handling outside Working too.
5. **Unknown outcomes:** FR8 says an in-flight atomic action shall finish and be verified, but timeouts can leave the outcome unknown. Bound waiting and specify when stop can be confirmed without falsely claiming an external operation completed.
6. **Delete wording:** FR.Sa.19 says every delete cannot be undone while also requiring recoverable trash where possible. Report the actual reversibility of the selected operation.
7. **Withdrawn approval:** FR20 / FR.Sa.15 should distinguish the unexecuted pending action from earlier completed work in the task. 'Nothing was done' can be false for a multistep task.
8. **Approval batches:** Specify membership, per-item identifiers, partial failures, cancellation of remaining items, and what a summary approves. Reconcile single-use approvals with multiple approved actions and P5.
